What changed:
- **Matched-pair input stage:** four identical units, A(vinp, vref) + B(vref, vinn) balanced against 2 × C(vfb, vref).
  - The gain ½ now comes from the unit count, not from R or gm.
  - The units' nonlinearity cancels as far as they match. This is the externally-linear idea using the square law instead of the exponential.
- **Miller caps:** thick-oxide PMOS accumulation caps replace the MOM caps. 18 × 18 µm gives the same crossover and phase margin as 31 × 31 µm MOM, with no metal layers used.
- **Area:** `d2s_mpdda` saves only 16% overall, because I spent area on matching and cascodes. A leaner variant that only swaps in the new input stage and caps halves the area (1238 µm²) but leaves current and offset as before.

Things that didn't work:
- **Resistor replacements:** a triode MOS, Sarpeshkar's well input and an undegenerated square-law pair all fail here. The units only cancel if their output depends on gp − gn alone, and these three depend on where the sources sit. Errors at full scale were 29 to 200 mV, against 1 mV with the resistor.
- **Cascoding the output while it is the ESD clamp:** not possible, because a cascode would put its own drain, not the clamp's, on the pad. Lengthening the internal cascodes is what gave the +12 dB.

**What I varied:** nothing. `d2s_mpdda` keeps the first version's output devices unchanged, and the proposal treats them as a fixed constraint to design around.

- **OP** is one P frame filled to 41 fingers per row: 82 × 6.66 µm at L = 0.6 µm. That is your PCell at ng = 41; IHP's largest P cell has 15 fingers per row.
- **ON** is two N frames with 33 fingers each, 66 × 4.4 µm, at **L = 1.0 µm**. Your `output_driver_sizing` study chose L = 1.0 for the NMOS's output resistance (Early voltage 9.7 V instead of 4 V). - **Built around them:**

  - I didn't cascode the output in case (a), because a stacked frame would be a new, unqualified ESD structure.
  - Case (b), `d2s_lc2`, uses 41 P and 33 N fingers as plain transistors in the slot.
- **Netlist limit:** the netlists only know W, L and ng. They model neither the wide drain nor the frame.

**What W/L PCells would add beyond your ng-only ones:**

1. **The current design becomes drawable.** The L = 1.0 µm N frame is assumed by both `d2s_miller` and `d2s_mpdda`. Your sizing data shows why it matters: with the gate fixed, the idle current changes 73/100/132 µA at L = 0.6 over the output range, and 87/100/112 µA at L = 1.0.

**The three devices**

| device | size | connection | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| MS1 | PMOS 0.5/100 | gate on vss, always on | weak pull-up of node ks |

## Oguey–Aebischer start-up

The core is a loop: the PMOS mirror (P0/P0C stacked diodes, copies P8, P2, PO) feeds the NMOS side (M11, MCS, and M10 through MCL), whose current comes back into the mirror through MC10 at vpc. Like any self-biased loop it has a second stable state at zero current. There, vbr and vnc are at 0 V and vpg and vpc float up near vdd.

The start-up devices are the same three as in `bg_core`, sensing vbr:

1. **Dead core:** vbr = 0 keeps MS2 off, so MS1 pulls ks up to vdd. At the same time MC10 and MCL are off, so nothing in the core can draw current from vpc.
2. **Kick:** MS3 turns on and pulls vpc down. Its current flows through P0C and P0, the same path M10's current takes in normal operation. vpg drops and every PMOS copy turns on.
3. **The core fills up:** P8 drives 8× into M11, so vnc rises. P2 drives 2× into MCS, so vbr rises. PO drives 20× into NI.
4. **The loop closes:** vbr turns on MCL (M10's source path) and MC10; vnc turns on M10. Now M10 and MC10 draw from vpc themselves. Below I0 the loop gain is above 1, so the core climbs on its own to I0, where the loop gain falls to 1.
5. **Release:** the same rise of vbr turns MS2 on. MS2 pulls ks down to 1.3 mV, which shuts MS3 off.

By the time vbr is high enough for MS2 to overpower MS1, the core is conducting and finishes the climb on its own. That held at every corner, for supply ramps of 1 µs and 100 µs.

Final operating point:

| node | voltage |
|---|---|
| vpg | 2.38 V |
| vpc | 1.11 V |
| vbr | 0.89 V |
| vnc | 0.54 V |
| d10 | 0.27 V |
| vres | 72 mV (U_T ln 16) |
| ks | 1.3 mV |

## What MC10 does

## Bulks of RP2 and RN2

The rule is that each replica's bulk goes where its counterpart's bulk goes in the driver. The bias stack has to reproduce the driver's class-AB loop, threshold voltages included:

- P side: V_SG(OP) + V_SG(ABP) = V_SG(RP1) + V_SG(RP2).
- N side: V_GS(ON) + V_GS(ABN) = V_GS(RN1) + V_GS(RN2).

| replica | counterpart | counterpart's bulk | so the replica's bulk goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| RP1 | OP | source on vddo | vddo, tied to its source |
| RP2 | ABP | n-well on vdd | vdd |
| RN1 | ON | local tap ring on vsso | vsso, tied to its source |
| RN2 | ABN | substrate, vss | vss |

RP2 sees the same body bias as ABP, even with an offset between the rails:
- ABP's source is at node a = vddo − V_SG(OP), so its well-to-source voltage is V_SG(OP) + (vdd − vddo).
- RP2's source is at n1 = vddo − V_SG(RP1), so its well-to-source voltage is V_SG(RP1) + (vdd − vddo).
- At matched current density V_SG(OP) = V_SG(RP1), so the two are equal.

RN2 and ABN match the same way on the N side.

I checked the alternatives at driver level, with the `in` bias:

| RP2 / RN2 bulk | I_Q | vddo −/+ 50 mV | vsso −/+ 50 mV |
|---|---|---|---|
| as drawn (RP2 well on vdd, RN2 bulk on vss) | 212 µA | 211 / 213 | 213 / 211 |
| RP2 well on its source n1 | 89 µA | 88 / 91 | 90 / 88 |
| RP2 well on vddo | 212 µA | 194 / 233 | 213 / 211 |
| RN2 bulk on its source n2 (would need an isolated p-well) | 107 µA | 106 / 108 | 111 / 104 |

- **Well tied to its source:** this removes the body effect that ABP has, about 0.7 V of source-to-well bias, so I_Q drops to less than half.
- **Well on vddo:** this matches only while vdd = vddo. A 50 mV offset between the rails then moves I_Q by about ±9 %, against ±0.5 % with the well on vdd.

A matched alternative would be to tie both ABP's and RP2's wells to their sources (node a and n1). That removes the body effect from the loop entirely. The cost is ABP's well capacitance on node a. On the N side the same option needs isolated p-wells, which I haven't checked are available in sg13cmos5l.
