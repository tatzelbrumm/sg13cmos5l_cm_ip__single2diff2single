## Findings that shape the questions

- **The bias needs more than the macro's `disable`.**
  - `ToBiasStartup`'s `disable` pulls only the NMOS lines (vbr, vbn) low. In our trees the PMOS lines would then float to about a threshold below vdd and still leak through their mirrors. To get to leakage only, vbp, vbpc and vabp also need pulling to vdd, and vbn and vbnc to vss.
  - In `_oa` and `_bg`, MS1 is always on and draws about 0.46 µA, so it needs switching off as well.
  - After enable, the core starts from the same all-off state the start-up circuit already handles. The open question is how quickly it starts from an enable step rather than a supply ramp.
- **The driver's gate nodes float when disabled.** With the bias off, ABP and ABN let go of a and b. So a needs a PMOS pulling it to vddo, and b an NMOS pulling it to vsso, both driven at 3.3 V.
  - Those switches sit on the two high-impedance nodes. Their capacitance touches phase margin, and their leakage at 125 °C touches offset. Checking this is part of "enabled performance unchanged".
- **Unpowered, nothing actively drives the gates.** Holding them there takes something passive, such as a large resistor from a to vddo and from b to vsso. But a resistor also loads a and b when the driver is running. Which tie is right depends on how IHP ties its clamp gates, so this waits for the `.cdl`.
- **vsso is still unassigned (vssio or vss_3v3).** Wherever it goes, ON's gate tie goes too. IHP ties its clamps to iovss, and vssio is the slot's ESD ground.

## The six questions, with the options I see

**1. Which signal enables the block**
- The slot's `enable`: it belongs to this slot only, is always valid, and is 1.2 V, active high.
- A `dig_in` bit: valid only while the slot is addressed and `dig_ena` is latched, and the harness RTL itself says that latch is unfinished.
- Both, ANDed together.
- There is no register at present (the scan chain question is reopened).
- Polarity and name: `enable`, active high, to match the slot, or `disable`, active high, to match `ToBiasStartup`.
- Port position: right after `vdd vss vddo vsso`, which fits the convention but shifts existing positional instances, or at the end of the list.

**2. Level shifting, and the state with vdd_1v2 off and vdd_3v3 on**
- A shifter inside each cell, or one shared shifter giving `en_3v3` and its complement.
- With vdd_1v2 off, fail-safe means disabled; the alternative is to keep running regardless of the digital side.

**3. Gate ties for case (a) only, or case (b) too**
- In case (a), OP and ON are the pad's clamps.
- In case (b), IOPadAnalog's own clamps protect the pad and OP/ON sit behind 587 Ω. The gates then only need to be off for zero current, and their unpowered state matters much less.

**4. Leakage budget at 27 °C and 125 °C**
- I expect OP (546 µm wide at L = 0.6) and ON at V_GS = 0 to dominate, more than the bias does. I'll measure this before quoting anything against a budget.

**5. Who switches off iref in variants 1 and 2**
- The harness, through `ibias_ena`.
- A series switch inside our cell.
- Both.
- If neither does, pulling the iref line low just means our cell sinks the external current.

**6. Wake-up time and vout during transitions**
- With both output devices off, vout floats.
- Releasing a and b before the loop has settled can kick vout toward a rail. The options are to accept a glitch within some bound, or to sequence the release: bias first, gates after.
- Wake-up target: still to be set. Measured so far, start-up follows the supply ramp, between 1 µs and 100 µs.
