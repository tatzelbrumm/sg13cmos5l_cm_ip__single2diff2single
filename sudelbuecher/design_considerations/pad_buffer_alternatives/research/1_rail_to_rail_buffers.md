# Search 1 — small, fast, wide-range class-AB buffer front ends for a 3.3 V / 130 nm pad driver (agent report, verbatim)

**Access note.** IEEE Xplore, ResearchGate, IET and academia.edu were unreachable from this session, so many numbers come from open-access copies or from the comparison tables printed in other papers (chiefly Grasso/Palumbo/Pennisi TCAS-II 2018 Table IV, Chen & Wang 2010 Table 1, Osmani et al. 2021, Piry et al. 2014). Where a number is second-hand I say so. "sim" = simulation-only result.

## Headline findings

1. **The LCD column-driver literature is the right place to look, but every design there is optimized for 0.6–1 nF loads and 1–10 µA.** Areas of 625–6600 µm² in 0.35 µm at 3–3.3 V are reported, i.e. already comparable to or smaller than your ~3500 µm² front end, in a process with ~2.7× coarser pitch. Scaled to 130 nm and re-biased for 100 pF, several-MHz loop bandwidth is plausible, but no paper in this family demonstrates >2 MHz closed-loop at 20–100 pF directly; you would be extrapolating gm/C.
2. **MOS-only, no-Miller-capacitor designs exist and work**: Grasso 2018 (dual push-pull, dominant pole at the load), Marano 2010, Arslan 2015/2018, Osmani 2021 (DFVF + inverter output, 10–11 transistors). They put the dominant pole on the load capacitor and keep the internal node low-impedance; the price is that bandwidth ∝ gm/C_L and the output devices get large (which your ESD-clamp class-AB stage gives you for free).
3. **Rail-to-rail input in this family is the Hogervorst-style complementary pair**, and in 0.35 µm/3.3 V it costs roughly 2–3 kµm² including the summing circuit — not the dominant cost. The real cost is the gm-control (3× mirrors or current switches); several LCD buffers simply omit gm control and accept gm doubling in the overlap region.
4. **FVF/SSF class-AB followers are the fastest per µA (tens of MHz at 10–20 pF), but they are not rail-to-rail at 3.3 V with Vt ≈ 0.7 V**, and with 100 pF the output pole collapses the usable loop gain unless gm is large (see the stability condition ω_pX > 2·GB in the FVF slides). Rail-to-rail versions need either a complementary pair with a switch, or quasi-floating-gate level shifters (= capacitors + GΩ pseudo-resistors), which conflicts with your MOS-only preference.
5. **A resistive 1 kΩ / 50 Ω load disqualifies pure single-stage OTAs** (DC gain collapses to gm·R_L); you need a low-impedance output stage, which you already have. The relevant question is only how to make the stage *before* it small and fast.

## Candidates, ranked

Ranking criteria: MOS-only first, then area, then bandwidth potential at 20–100 pF, then range.

### Tier 1 — MOS-only, no compensation capacitor, demonstrated small area

**1. Grasso, Palumbo, Pennisi, "Dual push-pull high-speed rail-to-rail CMOS buffer amplifier for flat-panel displays," TCAS-II 2018.** Complementary NMOS+PMOS input pairs (no gm control) feeding a multi-mirrored active load; output is a class-AB pair (MN2/MP1) biased from diode-connected devices plus a parallel class-B pair (MN1/MP2) switched on by two current comparators (MC1–MC4), with "current boosters" that forward-bias the class-B devices' bulks during transients so they can be smaller. Measured: 0.35 µm triple-well (ST BCD6), 3 V, **5562 µm²**, I_Q 1.63 µA, 1 nF load, 1 % settling 1.20/1.02 µs, SR 6.5/8.1 V/µs, UGBW 190 kHz at 1 nF, PM 53°, in/out 100 % V_DD. **No Miller capacitor** — dominant pole is the load. No resistors. Output devices 60×(1/0.35) and 30×(1/0.35). Limitation for you: UGBW scales as gm/C_L, so at 100 pF and 1.6 µA you'd be at ~2 MHz; reaching 10 MHz needs ~5× the input gm (tens of µA, still far below your 300 µA). Bulk-forward-bias boosters need triple-well (SG13 has it) and a body diode that tolerates ~0.4 V forward bias — check the PDK. Source: iris.unict.it PDF (see Sources).

**2. Marano, Palumbo, Pennisi, "Improved low-power high-speed buffer amplifier with slew-rate enhancement for LCD applications," JCSC 2010.** Per Grasso's Table IV: 0.35 µm, 3 V, class AB, **625 µm²** (smallest in the whole table), 8 µA, 1 nF, 0.8 µs, 100 % range. Topology detail not accessible to me (second-hand only); the sibling paper (Marano 2010, AICSP, "dual-path push-pull with quiescent current control") is 874.5 µm², 3.5 µA, 0.9 µs, and is the ancestor of #1. Treat as "existence proof that 0.6–0.9 kµm² in 0.35 µm is possible".

**3. Osmani, Arslan, Kiyak, "Rail-to-rail CMOS voltage buffer design for LCD column drivers," ICECET 2021 (sim).** Differential flipped-voltage-follower input (DFVF), common-source gain devices, class-AB inverter-type output, feedback to the DFVF inverting input; **11 transistors**, no compensation capacitor or resistor listed. 0.35 µm, 3.3 V, 72.6 × 40 µm = **2904 µm²**, I_Q 3.95 µA, 1 nF: 0.3 µs settling; **100 pF: 0.07 µs**; claimed −3 dB 22 MHz at 1 nF, 318 MHz unloaded (simulation, Microwind — treat the bandwidth claims skeptically; they are inconsistent with the settling times). In/out 100 % V_DD. Predecessor: Arslan, "Differential FVF based compact rail-to-rail buffer amplifier for LCD column drivers," EECS 2018: 10 transistors, 3.3 V, 4.45 µA, 0.4 µs at 1 nF, no area given. Limitation: DFVF input at 3.3 V/Vt 0.7 has an input common-mode window set by V_GS + V_DSAT stacks — "rail-to-rail" here is for the *output*; the paper does not analyse the input window at a 0.7 V threshold. Simulation only.

**4. Arslan & Yıldız, "A compact rail-to-rail CMOS buffer amplifier with very low quiescent current," Int. J. Electronics 2015 (post-layout sim).** Two complementary differential input stages with push-pull output devices hung directly on the differential stages, output taken from an inverter. AMS 0.35 µm, 3.3 V, **1380 µm²** (per Grasso Table IV), 1.6 µA, 1 nF, 1.45 µs, class B per Grasso's classification. No resistors/caps mentioned. Limitation: class-B crossover and low bandwidth as published; fine as a template, not as a spec.

**5. Yıldız, Minaei, Arslan, "High slew-rate low-quiescent current rail-to-rail CMOS buffer amplifier for flat panel displays," JCSC 2011.** Per Grasso Table IV: 0.35 µm, 3.3 V, class AB, **2160 µm²**, 3 µA, 600 pF, 0.85 µs, 100 %. Second-hand only.

**6. C.-W. Lu, "High-speed driving scheme and compact high-speed low-power rail-to-rail class-B buffer amplifier for LCD applications," JSSC 2004 (and Lu & Xiao, CCECE 2006).** Comparator-sensed class-B push-pull with rail-to-rail input; 0.35 µm, 3.3 V, **46.5 × 57 µm = 2650 µm²**, 7 µA, 600 pF, settling 2.7/2.9 µs, SR 4.5/4.2 V/µs, output 0–3.3 V (numbers via Chen & Wang 2010 Table 1). The 2006 CCECE version: 2528 µm², 5.4 µA, 1.5 µs (Grasso Table IV). Weng & Wu's original 2002 IEICE compact class-B: 6321 µm², 7.4 µA, 8 µs. These are the roots of the family; slower than the Palumbo/Pennisi designs.

### Tier 2 — MOS-only with a Miller capacitor, or needing special devices

**7. Chen & Wang, "Class-AB rail-to-rail CMOS buffer amplifier for TFT-LCD source drivers," J. Eng. NCHU 2010.** Complementary folded-cascode input + CMRR-enhancement second stage + class-AB output; Miller R_C/C_C (values not given). 0.35 µm, 3.3 V, 56 × 101 µm = **5656 µm²**, 3.1 µA, SR 61.5/43 V/µs, 0.2 % settling 1.84/1.34 µs into a 5 × (2 kΩ + 30 pF) ladder, output 0.011–3.296 V. Needs R_C — rank below.

**8. Grasso, Marano, Esparza-Alfaro, López-Martín, Palumbo, Pennisi, "Self-biased dual-path push-pull output buffer amplifier for LCD column drivers," TCAS-I 2014.** Rail-to-rail folded-cascode input, class-AB push-pull + auxiliary class-B pair driven by current comparators, Miller compensation through a current buffer (M11). 0.5/0.6 µm, 5 V, 6643 µm², 32 µA, 1 nF, 1 % settling 710/590 ns, PM ~50° at 30 pF / 88° at 1 nF. Needs a Miller cap; the current-buffer Miller is the right way to do it if you keep one.

**9. Kim, An, Kong, "High-speed column driver IC having buffer amplifier with embedded isolation switch and compact adaptive biasing," Electronics 2021 (and An & Kong 2020).** Rail-to-rail folded cascode, class-AB output, compensation capacitor C_C, and an adaptive-tail-current boost using **only two auxiliary transistors** (vs. 10 and 26 in prior adaptive-bias schemes). 1.6 µm 18 V HV process: 4402 µm² (21 % smaller than its 5580 µm² baseline), 8.5 kΩ ‖ 300 pF, 90 % falling settling 0.71 µs. The two-transistor adaptive bias is the transferable idea: it buys slew without the area of a comparator block.

**10. Wen et al., "High-speed low-power rail-to-rail buffer using dynamic-current feedback for OLED source driver applications," AICSP 2021.** Dual-output buffer, output stage cut off at rest, dynamic current bias; **compensation resistor** links the two outputs. 0.18 µm, 1.8/5 V, 52 × 59 µm = 3068 µm², 2.8 µA, 1 nF, 1.18 µs. Needs a resistor — ranked down.

### Tier 3 — rail-to-rail constant-gm op-amps (reference points for the cost of true rail-to-rail input)

**11. Hogervorst, Tero, Eschauzier, Huijsing, "A compact power-efficient 3 V CMOS rail-to-rail input/output operational amplifier for VLSI cell libraries," JSSC 1994.** Complementary input pairs with 3× current mirrors for gm control, folded-cascode summing, class-AB output with floating current source (same class-AB control principle as your present floating control). 1 µm BiCMOS (Vt 0.64 / −0.75 V, close to yours), 3 V, **0.04 mm²** (two versions), ~180 µA / 0.5 mW, UGF 2.6 MHz (Miller) / 6.4 MHz (cascoded Miller), PM 66°/53°, SR 2–4 / 4–8 V/µs, 10 pF ‖ 10 kΩ, 1 % settling 220/180 ns small-signal, 440/275 ns for 1 V step, max I_out 3 mA, offset ~5 mV, CMRR drops to ~43 dB in the gm takeover region. Two Miller caps; no resistors. Rail-to-rail input needs V_DD ≥ ~2.9 V for full range (it reports 2.5–2.9 V gives V_SS−0.4 to V_DD−1.4). Limitation for you: Miller caps, and 0.04 mm² at 1 µm is a 10× area penalty over Tier 1.

**12. de Langen & Huijsing, "Compact low-voltage power-efficient operational amplifier cells for VLSI," JSSC 1998.** Folded-mesh class-AB control with resistor-free minimum selector; PMOS-input (0.08 mm², 184 µA) and rail-to-rail (0.1 mm², 230 µA, current-switch gm control) versions, 1.6 µm CMOS, UGF 4 MHz, 5 pF ‖ 10 kΩ, SR 4–4.5 V/µs, 1 % settling 370–380 ns, offset varies ~8 mV across the rail-to-rail CM range. Miller caps. Same remarks as #11; the data point is that rail-to-rail input added 25 % area and 25 % current over PMOS-input.

**13. Zaidi, Grout, A'ain, "Rail-to-rail op-amp design incorporating negative Miller and Miller compensation," IJSEI 2018 (sim).** AMS 0.35 µm, 3.3 V, 120 × 121 µm ≈ 14,600 µm², 2.3 mW, 214 MHz UGF at 1 pF, CMIR 0–3.3 V, output 1.7 mV–3.295 V. Included only because its Table IV compares 3.3 V/0.35 µm rail-to-rail designs: Weng & Wu 2002 (0.75 MHz UGF at 600 pF), Zha & Yin 2015 (9.5 MHz, 0.96 mW). Not a candidate (power, two Miller caps, 1 pF load).

### Tier 4 — FVF / super-source-follower class-AB followers (fast, small, but range-limited at 3.3 V / Vt 0.7)

**14. Carvajal, Ramírez-Angulo, López-Martín, Torralba et al., "The flipped voltage follower: a useful cell for low-voltage low-power circuit design," TCAS-I 2005 (plus the authors' tutorial slides).** Shunt-feedback follower: R_out ≈ 1/(gm1·gm2·ro1), measured closed-loop R_out 20–100 Ω; GB = gm2/C_Y, non-dominant pole at the output node X, **stability requires ω_pX > 2·GB**, "the basic FVF usually requires compensation, especially with a cascode current mirror". Output-current asymmetry (sinks ≫ sources) is fixed by class-AB variants (FVF-NDP: >100 µA peak from 30 µA quiescent at 10 pF; FVF output stage in a 0.8 µm/1.5 V op-amp: 10 V/µs, 15 MHz UGF, 10 pF, 218 µA). Limitation: with 100 pF on node X and 1/gm1 as the open-loop resistance there, ω_pX = gm1/C_L; for 10 MHz GB you need gm1 ≳ 12 mS (my calculation from their criterion, not a paper number) — i.e. mA-class bias. Range: V_out = V_in − V_GS1, so a 1 V level shift and no rail-to-rail; the slides note a "high-voltage version adds a voltage shifter in the feedback loop".

**15. Piry, Khanjanimoaf, Amiri, "A new low-voltage, low-power and high-slew rate CMOS unity-gain buffer," IJEEE 2014 (sim).** FVF-based class-AB buffer with a transistor level shifter + capacitor (quasi-floating-gate style). 0.5 µm, 0.9 V, 16 µA, 36 µW, **52 MHz at 18 pF / 76 MHz at 10 pF**, SR 10.3/10.3 V/µs, R_out 210 Ω, THD −50 dB at 200 mVpp/1 MHz. The resistor variant needs ≥1 GΩ pseudo-resistor plus pF capacitor. Their Table 2 also lists the 3 V / 0.5 µm DFVF and CASDPFVF buffers [5]: 30 µA, 70 MHz at 10 pF, R_out 40 Ω and 0.5 Ω, THD −52 dB at 300 mVpp/2 MHz. Limitation: swings of a few hundred mV; no rail-to-rail; needs a capacitor and pseudo-resistor for the level shift.

**16. Bulk-driven super-source-follower rail-to-rail buffer (Westminster/Ramírez-Angulo group, "Class-AB rail-to-rail CMOS buffer with bulk-driven super source followers," sim).** Complementary pair of SSFs, bulk-driven input with replica bias to cancel the V_GS shift, quasi-floating-gate class-AB, a current switch hands over between the N and P halves. 0.35 µm, 1.8 V, <8 µA, 68 pF, THD −46 dB at 1.6 Vpp/100 kHz. No bandwidth or area given. Limitation: bulk-driven gm is ~0.2–0.3× gate gm, quasi-floating gates need caps + pseudo-resistors, and the N/P handover is a discontinuity in R_out.

**17. Class-AB level-shifted FVF (Thapar thesis, 180 nm, sim).** 750 MHz BW, R_out 107 Ω, but in/out swing only 1.01 V / 0.81 V — illustrates the swing ceiling of the FVF family.

**18. López-Martín, Baswa, Ramírez-Angulo, Carvajal, "Low-voltage super class-AB CMOS OTA cells with very high slew rate and power efficiency," JSSC 2005.** Single-stage OTA with adaptive-bias input (FVF-based) and local common-mode feedback for gain boosting. 0.5 µm, ±1 V, **80 pF load**, GBW 2.3–3.5 MHz, SR 10–20 V/µs (vs. 0.35 V/µs class A), PM 56–71°, 140–220 µW, **0.054 mm²** each. Relevant because it is the only measured single-stage class-AB cell here driving an 80 pF load at MHz; area is large because of 0.5 µm. Limitation: single-stage ⇒ no resistive-load capability; LCMFB uses resistors in the original (MOS-implementable, but then nonlinear).

### Tier 5 — single-stage / no-Miller OTAs for large C_L (class-AB current mirrors)

**19. Yan, Mak, Law, Martins (U. Macau), nested-current-mirror single-stage amplifier, ISSCC/JSSC 2014–15; Mak et al. "A hybrid OTA driving 15 nF capacitive load with 1.46 MHz GBW," JSSC 2015.** NCM: 180 nm, **0.0013 mm²**, 3.6 µW, rail-to-rail output, 0.15–15 nF, DC gain >66 dB, PM >62°. Hybrid OTA: 0.13 µm, 0.7 V, 24 µA, 2725 µm², 15 nF (per Grasso Table IV; 1.46 MHz GBW per title). No Miller cap. Limitation: tuned for nF loads; at 100 pF the same current gives ~10× the GBW but the mirror gain also multiplies the input-referred offset and noise; sub-1 V designs, so transistor stacking is not representative of 3.3 V/Vt 0.7 — in your process you have headroom to spare, which makes this family easier, not harder.

**20. Beloso-Legarra, de la Cruz Blas, López-Martín, "Power-efficient single-stage class-AB OTA based on non-linear nested current mirrors," TCAS-I 2023.** Split input pair in weak inversion with nonlinear NCM load; 180 nm, ±0.5 V, **0.001 mm²**, 2.9 µW, GBW 136 kHz at 160 pF, PM 90°, rail-to-rail output, "no additional circuit elements". Same remarks; a 3.3 V strong-inversion version would be ~100× faster per the gm/C scaling.

**21. Itakura & Minamizaki, "A two-gain-stage amplifier without an on-chip Miller capacitor in an LCD driver IC," IEICE 2002.** Two gain stages, compensation by a zero formed by the load capacitance and a series resistor between amplifier output and load; ~5 µA; chip area −30–40 % by dropping a >1000 pF Miller cap. Needs a resistor and assumes the load is capacitive — ranked low, but it is the canonical "use the load as compensation" reference.

**22. Li et al., "A complementary recycling OTA with data-driven enhancement of transconductance," Electronics 2019.** Fully differential complementary RFC, 0.18 µm, 5 V, 0.42 × 0.32 mm², 530/1320 µA, 17.5 MHz (static) / 49–83 MHz (boosted), 18.4 pF, SR 10/55 V/µs; uses resistors in the local CMFB. Not small — listed only as a 5 V/0.18 µm data point for what a complementary folded-cascode front end costs when done without area discipline.

## What the comparison tables say about area vs. topology (0.35 µm, 3–3.3 V, 1 nF class)

From Grasso 2018 Table IV (areas in µm², all measured or post-layout, class AB unless noted): Marano'10 **625** (8 µA), Marano'10 AICSP **874** (3.5 µA), Marano'10 class-B 915, Arslan'15 class-B 1380 (1.6 µA), Wang'07 2115 (7.7 µA), Yıldız'11 2160 (3 µA), Lu'06 class-B 2529, Lu'11 class-B 2940, Grasso'18 5562 (1.63 µA, no Miller cap), Weng'02 class-B 6321, Huang'11 (distributed Miller) 6588, Grasso'14 (Miller + current buffer) 6643 (32 µA). Pattern: designs with Miller compensation cluster at 5.5–6.6 kµm²; the no-capacitor comparator/push-pull designs cluster at 0.6–2.2 kµm². No paper explicitly compares two-stage-Miller vs single-stage area at matched specs; this table is the closest proxy.

## Implications for your case (my analysis, flagged as such)

- Your front end's dominant cost is the four 150 kΩ degeneration resistors, the 3–6 µm long-L folded cascode and two 1 pF Miller caps. All Tier-1 designs drop all three: short-L input pairs, mirrors instead of cascodes, dominant pole on the load.
- Bandwidth budget for a load-compensated front end: GBW ≈ gm_in·A_mirror/(2π·C_L). For 10 MHz at 100 pF you need an effective gm of ~6 mS; with a 3–5× class-AB mirror gain that's ~1.5 mS of input gm, ~100–200 µA in strong inversion at 3.3 V — less than your present 300 µA. With 20 pF it is trivial.
- Resistive loads: the Tier-1 topologies all terminate in a low-impedance class-AB inverter-type stage, which is exactly your ESD-clamp stage; 1 kΩ is covered by the examples (Kim 2021 drives 8.5 kΩ; Chen & Wang drive a 2 kΩ ladder). None drives 50 Ω; a 50 Ω load at ±0.5 V needs ±10 mA peaks and your clamp devices would have to supply it with the loop gain intact.
- Rail-to-rail input: the cheapest route in this literature is the un-gm-controlled complementary pair (Grasso 2018, Arslan 2015). Your input window problem (gates ≥1.3 V below V_DD) disappears with an added NMOS pair; the gm doubling in mid-range costs phase margin, not area. Hogervorst's 3× mirrors are the fix if you need constant loop gain.
- FVF/SSF: only worth it if you accept a ~1 V level shift and a sub-2 V output window; at 100 pF the FVF's own stability criterion pushes you to mA-class gm. Not recommended for this load.
